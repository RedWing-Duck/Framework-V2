SCRIPT — Week 01: THIS PLACE HAS A BIG FOOTPRINT
Retroment Gaming | Narrative Writer pass
Source of truth: GDD Week 01. IDs below map 1:1 to GDD IDs.
Voice: second person, present tense. TOUR = quippy noir.
ESCAPE = tense thriller.
Room text keys: FIRST (first TOUR visit), REVISIT (later TOUR
visits), ESCAPE (every visit once ESCAPE has begun; exits named
by landmark only, per GDD section 7).

==============================================================
ROOMS
==============================================================

[R1] Private Elevator
FIRST:
The elevator doors open on a box of mirrored gold, and you
see six versions of Walter Pryce, bookstore investor, all of
them sweating a little. Don Chava's man hits the button and
steps back out. The only way is north, into the foyer, where
a voice is already saying your name like it's a deal.
REVISIT:
Mirrored gold, and your reflection trying hard to look like
it belongs here. The foyer is north.
ESCAPE:
The gold box hums, waiting. One button, one way down. Behind
you, the double staircase and every problem you left on it.

[R2] Grand Foyer
FIRST:
White marble floors, gold banisters, and two staircases that
curve up to the same landing like arms around a hug you
didn't ask for. The left staircase is wide and carpeted; the
right is narrower, polished to a shine. The trophy room
glitters west, a piano waits in the salon east, a long table
stretches north in the dining hall, the stairs lead up, and
the elevator is south.
REVISIT:
The twin staircases, left for family and right for business,
still curling up toward the landing. West to the trophies,
east to the piano, north to dinner, up the stairs, or south
to the elevator.
ESCAPE:
The foyer lights flicker. Somewhere above, glass breaks. The
two staircases rise into the dark; the right one gleams, its
thirteenth step catching the emergency light. The gold
elevator waits behind you. The trophy cases glitter off to
one side, the piano salon to the other, the long dinner table
straight ahead.

[R3] Trophy Room
FIRST:
Glass cases line the walls, lit like jewelry counters. Inside:
a GOLD RIFLE engraved with roses, a pistol studded with enough
diamonds to buy a small town, and a framed newspaper headline
someone has carefully cut the name out of. The foyer is back
east.
REVISIT:
The glass cases, the GOLD RIFLE, the diamonds, the headline
with the missing name. East to the foyer.
ESCAPE:
The cases rattle each time something heavy hits a floor above.
The GOLD RIFLE stares back at you, useless. The only way out
is back toward the staircases.

[R4] Music Salon
FIRST:
A white grand PIANO sits in the middle of the room like the
only guest who got a chair. Its lid is a sheet of mirror, and
it shows you the chandelier, the ceiling fresco, and your own
nervous smile. The foyer is west.
REVISIT:
The mirrored PIANO, reflecting the chandelier and your
nerves. West to the foyer.
ESCAPE:
The PIANO waits in the half-light, its mirrored lid reflecting
flashing alarm lights. Mama's piano. Mama's song. The way out
is back toward the staircases.

[R5] Dining Hall
FIRST:
A table long enough to seat a jury, set for twenty. Two
portraits face each other across it: a stern woman in a
rebozo and a sterner woman holding a wooden spoon. Chili,
garlic, and slow-cooked tomato hang in the air. The foyer is
south.
REVISIT:
The endless table, the two grandmothers watching each other
like rival dons. South to the foyer.
ESCAPE:
Chairs knocked over, a lemur on the table eating grapes out
of a silver bowl. It doesn't care about you. The way out is
back toward the staircases.

[R6] Upper Landing
FIRST:
Both staircases meet here at a balcony that looks down on the
foyer. A gallery of portraits stretches west, and a heavy
office door stands open to the east, leaking cigar smoke. The
stairs lead back down.
REVISIT:
The balcony where the two staircases meet. West to the
portraits, east to the office, down to the foyer.
ESCAPE:
The balcony shakes under a distant thud. Cigar smoke still
drifts from the office. The hall of portraits stretches one
way; the staircases drop down to the foyer.

[R7] Gallery Hall
FIRST:
A long hall of oil portraits, all of them the Don: the Don on
a horse, the Don with a lion, the Don as a saint. The
wallpaper is peacock feathers. A guard passes by and nods.
The atrium is north, the landing east.
REVISIT:
A hundred painted Don Chavas watch you walk. North to the
atrium, east to the landing.
ESCAPE:
The painted Dons glare down, and one portrait is hanging
crooked, torn by claws. Chlorine drifts in from the fountain
behind you. Ahead, the balcony at the top of the stairs.

[R8] Atrium
FIRST:
A glass roof, tropical plants, and in the center a bronze
HIPPO fountain spouting water from its nostrils with great
dignity. Its mouth is closed tight. The gallery is south and
a chorus of shrieking birds comes from the north.
REVISIT:
The bronze HIPPO spouts water, mouth shut. South to the
gallery, north to the birds.
ESCAPE:
Water sloshes over the fountain's edge. Lemurs chatter in the
palms, pelting the floor with fruit. The bronze HIPPO waits,
mouth shut. The screaming aviary is behind you; ahead, the
hall of portraits.

[R9] The Don's Office
FIRST:
Leather, mahogany, a humidor the size of a coffin, and a
window that looks down on a city he probably owns part of.
A framed sign on the desk reads "LEGITIMATE BUSINESS." The
landing is west.
REVISIT:
The leather, the cigars, the sign that protests too much.
West to the landing.
ESCAPE:
Papers everywhere, a chair knocked over, the humidor open and
empty. Whatever mattered in here, the Don took it. The only
way out is back to the balcony.

[R10] Aviary
FIRST:
A glass room full of peacocks that scream like car alarms
with feelings. The AVIARY door has a keypad lock, glowing
red. The atrium is south, and fresh night air blows in from
the terrace north.
REVISIT:
The peacocks, the screaming, the red keypad on the AVIARY
door. South to the atrium, north to the terrace.
ESCAPE:
The AVIARY keypad is smashed, its door rattling. The peacocks
are screaming louder than ever. The fresh air of the terrace
is behind you; the chlorine smell of the fountain is ahead.

[R11] Zoo Terrace
FIRST (this is the moment ESCAPE begins):
A rooftop garden with a view of the whole city. In a tall yard
a giraffe chews thoughtfully; beside it, a black jaguar paces
behind the JAGUAR GATE. Then the alarms start. Lemurs pour out
of the dark, a roar shakes the glass, and the Don goes pale:
"The accountant. He opened the big one." He's gone south with
his guards in three seconds flat. You are alone, holding a
plaque proposal, with something enormous waking up to the
east.
REVISIT (ESCAPE only):
Wind, alarms, and the giraffe peering over the GIRAFFE GATE.
The jaguar paces behind the JAGUAR GATE. The broken enclosure
gapes to the east; the screaming aviary is behind you.

[R12] Broken Enclosure
ESCAPE (only reachable in ESCAPE):
Steel bars bent outward like someone opened a bag of chips.
A brass sign reads "BIG TONY — DO NOT FEED." Footprints the
size of snow shovels lead back past you. The only way out is
the terrace.

ITEMS
==============================================================

[I1] BRIEFING CARD
LOOK: Your cover, in your own handwriting. Name: Walter Pryce.
Business: The Dog-Eared Page, a bookstore planned for the
empty lot next door. Offer: a brass plaque reading "The
Bellandi-Reyes Reading Room." At the bottom: "Smile. Don't
touch anything."
TAKE: You already have it. It's the only thing keeping you
from being a very short story.
USE (success): Not needed; the card is for reading. (Engine:
there is no use-on target for I1.)
USE (wrong target): That won't help. The card is for reading,
not for handing to anyone.

[I2] GOLD COIN
LOOK: A heavy gold coin with an eagle on one side and a winged
figure on the other. The Don called it your "first dividend."
TAKE: You pocket the coin. It's warm from his hand, which
somehow makes it worse.
USE on HIPPO (success): see P2 SOLVED.
USE (wrong target): You hold out the coin. Nothing here
wants it. You put it back before you lose it.

[I3] LEDGER
LOOK: A fat notebook held shut with rubber bands, full of
initials, dates, and payments in neat columns. Two pages list
nothing but rubber band purchases. Someone had a lot of cash
to wrap.
TAKE: You tuck the ledger inside your jacket. Secret one.
USE (success): Not needed. It's evidence; just get it out.
USE (wrong target): Tempting, but this is evidence, not a
tool. Keep it safe.

[I4] SHIPPING MANIFEST
LOOK: A waterproof tube holding a shipping schedule: dates,
ports, and thousands of cans of "premium chiles" that clearly
have never been near a chile.
TAKE: The manifest goes into your jacket. Secret two.
USE (success): Not needed. It's evidence; just get it out.
USE (wrong target): Tempting, but this is evidence, not a
tool. Keep it safe.

[I5] DEAL PHOTOS
LOOK: A stack of photos: the Don shaking hands on a yacht with
men whose faces would end careers. Someone wrote a date on
the back of each one.
TAKE: You slide the photos into your jacket. Secret three.
USE (success): Not needed. It's evidence; just get it out.
USE (wrong target): Tempting, but this is evidence, not a
tool. Keep it safe.

NPCs

[N1] DON CHAVA (Salvatore Bellandi-Reyes)
GREETING (on entering R2, first visit):
"Mr. Pryce! Welcome. Salvatore Bellandi-Reyes, but friends
call me Chava, and you're a friend until you're not." He
grips your hand like he's weighing it.

MAIN DIALOGUE, by tour stop (plays on entering; the final line
of each stop names the direction of the next one):

R2 (foyer):
"Two staircases, two families. My mother's people came from
the mountains of the west coast, my father's from Palermo. In
this house we never choose between family." He taps the left
banister. "Left stairs, twelve steps, for family. Right
stairs, thirteen, for business. Never trust the thirteenth
step, amigo." He winks. "Come, the trophy room. West."

R3 (trophy room):
"My collection. Every piece has a story, and I tell none of
them." He nods at the GOLD RIFLE. "Admire. Don't touch. Back
east, then the salon."

R4 (music salon):
"My mother's piano. Only one song is ever played on her: my
mother's song. Anything else is disrespect." He watches your
hands. "Now, dinner. Back west, then north."

R5 (dining hall):
"Mama Rosa sang 'Cielito Lindo' every Sunday at this table.
Every Sunday. My nonna banged her spoon on the table to keep
time." Then he leans in. (Q1 follows; see Q1 below.)
After Q1 is answered correctly: "Good answer. Now upstairs,
to talk business. South, then up, then east."

R9 (office):
He drops into a leather chair. "A reading room. With my name
on it. You know what that does for a man's reputation?" He
smiles. (Q2 follows; see Q2 below.)
After Q2 is answered correctly: He flips you a GOLD COIN.
"Your first dividend, partner. Now let me show you my real
investments. West, west, then north."

R7 (gallery):
"Me, by the finest painters money can scare." A guard hurries
past. "My boys know the rule: the animals come first. Anything
gets loose, they drop everything and chase it. North, to my
girl."

R8 (atrium):
"Pepita." He flicks a gold coin into the HIPPO's mouth, and the
bronze jaw swings open, then snaps shut. "She only opens up for
gold. Like my accountants. North, the birds."

R10 (aviary):
"My peacocks. Loud, beautiful, and they don't know how to
lie. North. Time you met the family."

AFTER TASK (not applicable after ESCAPE begins; the Don is
gone): "Talk business later, Walter. Keep walking."
TALK DURING TOUR (repeat): "Questions later, Walter. Keep up."

[N3] BIG TONY (Bigfoot)

PUZZLES
==============================================================

[P1] Mama's Song (R4)
SETUP: (in R4 FIRST and ESCAPE text) The mirrored PIANO.
HINT: He told you whose song this piano keeps. He told you
the title over dinner.
SOLVED (play CIELITO LINDO, ESCAPE):
You play the first bars of "Cielito Lindo," badly but with
feeling. Something clicks. The mirrored lid rises on its own,
and a hidden tray slides out holding a LEDGER bound in rubber
bands. Secret one.
WRONG ATTEMPT (play PIANO, or any other song, ESCAPE):
You bang out a few notes. The piano does nothing, but you're
pretty sure Mama Rosa is disappointed. Which song does this
piano keep?
TOUR ATTEMPT: see SYSTEM > TOUR STRIKES.

[P2] Pepita's Price (R8)
SETUP: (in R8 text) The bronze HIPPO, mouth shut.
HINT: The hippo has expensive taste. So did the Don's
handshake.
SOLVED (use GOLD COIN on HIPPO, ESCAPE):
You flick the coin into Pepita's mouth. The bronze jaw swings
open, and a waterproof tube tumbles out into the water. You
fish out the SHIPPING MANIFEST. Secret two.
WRONG ATTEMPT (open HIPPO, push HIPPO, etc., ESCAPE):
You pull at the bronze jaw. Pepita's got a grip like a bank
vault. She only opens up for one thing.
TOUR ATTEMPT: see SYSTEM > TOUR STRIKES.

[P3] The Thirteenth Step (R2)
SETUP: (in R2 text) The right staircase, polished, one step
catching the light.
HINT: Business has one more step than family.
SOLVED (push THIRTEENTH STEP, ESCAPE):
You press the thirteenth step. It sinks with a click, and a
panel in the staircase slides open, releasing cold air from a
narrow passage. Just inside, a shoebox holds the DEAL PHOTOS.
Secret three.
WRONG ATTEMPT (push other steps, push STAIRCASE, etc., ESCAPE):
You press a step. It's just a step. The Don said which one
you can't trust.
TOUR ATTEMPT: see SYSTEM > TOUR STRIKES.

SYSTEM
==============================================================

INTRO SCREEN:
THIS PLACE HAS A BIG FOOTPRINT
A Retroment Gaming production.

Your client wants three secrets from Don Chava's penthouse. Your
cover: Walter Pryce, bookstore investor, carrying a plaque proposal
and a smile. The Don will give you the grand tour himself.
Listen to everything. Touch nothing. Get out with at least two.

Type HELP for commands.

HELP TEXT:
Move: north, south, east, west, up, down (or n, s, e, w, u, d).
Look around: look. Look at something: examine [thing] (or x).
Take, drop, use [item] on [thing], open, push, play, read.
Talk: talk to [person]. Answer: say [words].
Other: inventory (i), status, hint, quit.
On the tour, free actions: look, examine, inventory, status,
help, hint. Once the chaos starts, every other command costs
time.

STATUS:
TOUR: "Tour in progress. Strikes: [x]/3."
ESCAPE: "Bigfoot clock: [x] turns. Secrets: [y]/3. Run: [ready /
used]. Alibis: [list of released, unused animals, or none]."

HINT (general, no puzzle in room):
TOUR: "Listen to the Don. Every brag is a clue."
ESCAPE: "Walk the tour backward. Grab what you can. The guards
chase animals first."

"I DON'T UNDERSTAND THAT" (3 variations, rotate):
1. "You try that. The universe gives you a look usually
   reserved for bad checks."
2. "That doesn't make sense, even for a guy pitching a
   bookstore to a kingpin."
3. "Nice try, detective. Say it plain."

TOUR BLOCK (exit not on the tour):
"The Don puts a hand on your shoulder. 'Private, amigo. This
way.'"

--- TOUR QUESTIONS ---
Q1 (R5) PROMPT:
"So tell me, Walter. Sunday dinner: my mother's pozole or my
nonna's Sunday gravy?"
Q1 CORRECT (say BOTH):
He laughs and slaps the table. "Both! Now you sound like
family."
Q1 WRONG: (adds a strike; he asks again)
"You'd choose? In this house?" He asks again, slower.

Q2 (R9) PROMPT:
"Remind me, what's your bookstore called again?"
Q2 CORRECT (say DOG-EARED PAGE):
"The Dog-Eared Page. Cute. I like cute." See N1 R9 after Q2.
Q2 WRONG: (adds a strike; he asks again)
"That's not what you told my people." He asks again, not
smiling.

--- TOUR STRIKES ---
(Strike objects: GOLD RIFLE, PIANO, THIRTEENTH STEP / STAIRCASE,
HIPPO. Examine is free and safe.)
STRIKE 1:
The Don's smile tightens. "Hands to yourself, Walter. That's a
collector's piece."
STRIKE 2:
The smile is gone. "Twice now. I don't like three."
STRIKE 3: -> ending L1.

GATES DURING TOUR (open AVIARY / GIRAFFE GATE / JAGUAR GATE):
"The keypad blinks red. Locked tight, and the Don is watching."
(Not a strike.)

--- ESCAPE: ALIBI PREP ---
open AVIARY (R10):
You punch the smashed keypad. The door swings open and peacocks
explode into the palace, screaming like the world is ending. Perfect alibi:
PEACOCKS.
open GIRAFFE GATE (R11):
You lift the latch. The giraffe ducks out and ambles off toward
the stairs, curious about everything. Perfect alibi: GIRAFFE.
open JAGUAR GATE (R11):
You swing the gate open and flatten yourself against the wall.
The jaguar pours past like smoke and disappears downstairs.
Perfect alibi: JAGUAR.
Already open: "It's already open. That animal's somebody else's
problem now."

--- ESCAPE: GUARD CHECKPOINTS ---
TRIGGER: see N2 GREETING.
RUN (first time):
You bolt. Nando grabs for you, and a lemur lands on his head.
By the time he pulls it off, you're long gone.
say PEACOCKS (released, unused):
"The Don wants the peacocks rounded up! Do you hear them?"
Nando does. He sprints toward the screaming.
say GIRAFFE (released, unused):
"The giraffe's loose on the staircase! Boss said animals
first!" Nando swears and runs.
say JAGUAR (released, unused):
"The JAGUAR is out. It went that way." Nando goes very pale
and very fast.
ANYTHING ELSE (including a second run, an unreleased animal, or
an alibi already used): -> ending L2.

--- ESCAPE: BIGFOOT CLOCK ---
6 TURNS LEFT:
Somewhere behind you, a thud rattles the chandeliers.
3 TURNS LEFT:
A roar, closer now. Something is sniffing its way through the
palace.
1 TURN LEFT:
The floor shakes. You smell wet dog and bad decisions. Move.
0: -> ending L3.

--- ELEVATOR (R1, ESCAPE) ---
"down" with fewer than 2 secrets:
Your thumb hovers over the button. Two secrets minimum, or this
was all for nothing. You're not leaving empty-handed.
"down" during TOUR:
"Leaving before dessert?" The Don steers you back out.

ENDINGS
==============================================================

[W1] CLEAN GETAWAY (2 secrets)
The elevator doors slide shut on the roaring, screaming,
honking chaos above. Floors tick by. You pat your jacket: two
secrets, safe. One is still up there, but tonight, two was
plenty. The doors open onto the street, the city, and the
empty lot next door. It would make a great bookstore.
THE END. Secrets recovered: 2/3.

[W2] CLEAN SWEEP (3 secrets)
The doors shut. Your jacket is so heavy with evidence it
practically has a credit rating. Ledger, manifest, photos.
Every secret the Don had, and he gave you the tour himself.
Somewhere upstairs, a giraffe is eating the plaque proposal.
THE END. Secrets recovered: 3/3. Perfect run.

[L1] COVER BLOWN (3 strikes)
The Don goes very still. "Walter. If that's your name." He
snaps his fingers, and two guards appear. "Show our friend the
wine cellar. He likes to touch things. Let him touch the door
from the inside."
GAME OVER.

[L2] CAUGHT (wrong answer at a checkpoint)
Nando doesn't buy it. A heavy hand, a plastic zip tie, and a
long walk back to the Don. "An investor," the Don says. "I'm
so disappointed."
GAME OVER.

[L3] BIG FOOTPRINT (clock hits 0)
A shadow the size of a vending machine fills the hall. A hand
like a catcher's mitt lifts you off your feet, gently, the way
you'd pick up a kitten, and carries you back to the broken
enclosure to keep him company. Big Tony is lonely. Big Tony is
also very, very strong.
GAME OVER.

GAME-OVER PROMPT (after any L ending):
"Type RESTART to try again, or QUIT to walk away."

AFTER THE GAME ENDS (any input except RESTART/QUIT):
"The story's over, detective. RESTART or QUIT."
